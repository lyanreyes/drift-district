Tyres: on the circuits pick Soft (fastest, wears out in about 3 laps), Medium (about 5 laps), Hard (slower, lasts most of a race) or Wet, with the round buttons or keys 1–4. Before the start the choice goes on straight away; during a race it goes on at your next pit stop.

Rain: the host can set the weather to dry, rain or changeable. A wet track has much less grip and longer braking. On the three Grand Prix circuits, slick tyres are very slippery in the rain, so pit for wets — and pit again for slicks when it dries.

Pit stops: on the Grand Prix circuits your tyres wear out and lose grip, and crashes damage the car and cost top speed. Drive into the pit lane on the inside of the main straight and stop in a yellow box for 2.5 seconds to get fresh tyres and repairs. A wrecked car is put back on track after a short delay.

Penalties: circuit races are 10 laps. Running off the track at speed gives a warning, and the third one is a 5-second penalty. Ramming another car from behind also costs 5 seconds. The pit lane has an automatic 100 km/h limiter. Penalties are added to your finish time.

Off-road: the rally car, 4x4 pickup and monster truck keep their grip and speed on grass and dirt, so they suit the Rally Stage. The monster truck takes far less crash damage.

Drift District is a fan-made, non-commercial game for playing with friends. It is not affiliated with any racing series or team. Engine recordings: racing engine loops by domasx2 (OpenGameArt, CC0) and a Rover V8 recording by Collard (Wikimedia Commons, public domain).
